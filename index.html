Our Story

Rooted in Kunzer, worn everywhere.

Kunzer sits thirty kilometres from Srinagar, on the banks of the Ferozpora Nallah — a small town known for its clean spring water and, quietly, for the needlework of the families who live there.

YAWUN began as an effort to keep that needlework in daily use rather than in museum cases: pherans, shawls and kurtas made the way they have always been made, then worn by people far from the valley.

Today YAWUN is a boutique-cum-showroom in Kunzer — walk in for a ready piece, or bring your own fabric and design for custom stitching or custom embroidery made to your measurements.

Every piece passes through a single artisan's hands from thread to finish. We work in small batches, because sozni and tilla embroidery cannot be rushed without it showing.

The Craft

Three techniques, one valley.

Each garment is built around a technique chosen for the fabric and the occasion it's made for — not applied as decoration afterward.

Sozni

Fine needle embroidery worked with a single thread, used for the dense floral panels seen on formal pherans and shawls.

"the finest of the needle"

Tilla

Metallic thread couched onto fabric in bold geometric and paisley motifs — the technique reserved for bridal and festive wear.

"gold laid, not stitched"

Aari

A hooked needle pulls chain-stitch loops in continuous lines, giving everyday kurtas and stoles their running vine patterns.

"the hook that draws"
